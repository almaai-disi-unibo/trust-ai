// About the seminar

From principles to practical evidence.

AI governance is often discussed in terms of high-level principles, legal obligations, or policy choices. Yet, effective governance also requires practical environments in which these requirements can be interpreted, tested, and refined together with technical developers, regulators, affected stakeholders, and civil society. This seminar examines AI regulatory sandboxes as such environments: structured experimental settings that can help translate the EU AI Act and broader international governance principles into concrete practices for trustworthy AI development.

Drawing on European experiences, including the EUSAiR and AEQUITAS projects, and ongoing work on trustworthy AI and compliance within the IT4LIA AI Factory, the seminar will discuss how sandboxes can support legal certainty and innovation while enabling the testing of risk-management, fairness, transparency, human-oversight, and data-governance measures. It will also address their potential role in science diplomacy: creating shared learning spaces across jurisdictions, connecting technical and legal expertise, and making AI governance more evidence-based, interoperable, and inclusive.

// International seminar series 2026–2028

Science diplomacy and global AI governance.

This seminar is part of the 2026–2028 international seminar series of the DEEPLO Chair in Science Diplomacy and Global AI Governance, led by Catherine Régis at the Université de Montréal, in collaboration with Alma AI at the University of Bologna and TRUST-AI.

The Bologna session explores how interdisciplinary collaboration and shared experimental spaces can connect governance principles, technical evidence and trustworthy innovation.
